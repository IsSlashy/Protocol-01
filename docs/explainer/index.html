How the link survived — and how our own test missed it

devnet · 2026-08-18 05:38 UTC · probe P11
Step 1 of 6 · the promise

Buy a subscription without anyone knowing it was you

The subscription itself is public — anyone can see that it exists and that it was paid for. What has to stay hidden is who bought it.

So only one question matters: can a stranger start from the subscription and walk backwards, on the public ledger, all the way to your wallet?

Yesterday the answer was yes. It took a few minutes.

Step 2 of 6 · the detour

We hid the name by adding two hops

Think of posting an anonymous letter. You don't want your handwriting on the envelope, so you hand it to a courier with some cash and he posts it for you.

That is what these two middle boxes are. Your wallet never touches the subscription. Two separate payments — and neither one of them carries both your name and the destination.

That sounds like it works. It doesn't.

Step 3 of 6 · the false green

Our own test said PASS

We have an automated checker for exactly this. It opened three things: the subscription itself, and the histories of the two accounts that directly paid for it.

It never opened the middleman's history.

And the middleman's address was printed on screen, in plain text, by another part of the same tool. We handed ourselves the key and never turned the lock.

Step 4 of 6 · the walk

The courier keeps a logbook

One line above the delivery it reads: "received cash from [your name], 10:31."

Anyone can read the middleman's history. In it, one line before it funds the throwaway account, sits a payment signed by your wallet.

Cost of finding this: 28 database lookups. No hacking, no special access, no insider. Just curiosity.

Step 5 of 6 · the real lesson

A detour moves your name. It doesn't remove it.

And the clock finishes the job: the money went in one second before it went out, for exactly the amount the deposit costs.

No amount of cryptography survives that. The timing puts back together what the structure took apart.

One more thing worth saying out loud: a cruder test had flagged this, and I dismissed it as noise. When one tool says "I found a link" and another says "there is no link", believe the one that found something.

Step 6 of 6 · the fix

Two accounts instead of one

Today, one single account does both jobs — it takes your money and it pays for the private side. That one fact is the entire leak.

Split it in two, and the chain breaks for real.

We also fixed the checker — it now opens the middleman's history, and it refuses to say "pass" when it can't see everything. And the live system now blocks any purchase that would create this link, which means today's checkout gets rejected. That isn't a regression. That's the check working.
