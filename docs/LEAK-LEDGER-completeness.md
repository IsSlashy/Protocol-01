I verified the gaps I could settle from the tree. Here is what is **not** in the inventory, ordered by what it costs to find out late.

---

**1. Recovering C0 or C1 retroactively voids C7's mask for the same identity — no entry states the composition.**
The ledger has `c0-pause-resume-secret` (C0's witness is the note secret, recoverable by plain Lagrange) and `proof-bytes-c1-witness-recovery` (all four C1 inputs recovered today) as separate rows, and `c7-trace-column-channel` as a third. Nobody joined them. C7's witness *is* that same secret: `stark/src/air/spend.rs:920` takes `secret: BaseElement` and `:1052` computes `nullifier = run_hash(..., nullifier_preimage, secret)`. So for any identity that has ever paused a subscription (C0, `pause_private_stark.rs:104`) or spent a v3 note (C1, still registered), the C7 witness is *known*, and the 128 mask rows protect nothing — an observer holding the secret recomputes the commitment, matches the leaf, and can spend the note. The mask defends against an observer who must *solve*; it does nothing against one who was handed the answer by another circuit over the same value. **Why nobody listed it:** each sweeper owned one surface, and this channel is a join across two circuits that belongs to neither. **Measurement:** take one identity that has both a C0 pause and a C7 spend on devnet, run the existing `witness_recovery_positive_control.rs` Lagrange step on the C0 bytes, then check whether the recovered value reproduces the C7 spend's published nullifier via `poseidon::hash2(np, secret)`. A match ends the C7 privacy claim for that user, and it is a fund-loss finding, not a privacy one.

**2. C7's hold column carries nine values derived from the secret, and the harness models them as nine free unknowns when they are one.**
`stark/src/air/spend.rs:1152-1157`: for `cycle in 3..FIRST_FREE_CYCLE`, `let filler = poseidon::hash2(secret, BaseElement::new(cycle as u64))`, written across rows 96..383 of column 9. The comment at `:1137-1147` is honest — "⚠️ THIS IS NOT MASKING AND MUST NOT BE CALLED THAT … Thirteen values derived from one seed is one degree of freedom." But no ledger entry carries it. `hold-column-commitment-residue` covers rows 0..95 (the commitment) and is marked closed by the coset; the filler in rows 96..383 is a *different* region, is a deterministic function of the note secret, and is inside the column the whole ZK argument is about. Two consequences nobody has priced: (a) `air_aware_recovery_c7.rs` builds a 138-unknown model (1 hold + 9 filler + 128 mask) against 90 equations — but the 9 filler values and the 1 hold value are all determined by `secret`, so the true unknown count is nearer 130, and the margin the founder is being asked to engrave was computed on a model deliberately looser than the wire; (b) the filler is **invariant across every proof of the same note**, because it is a pure function of the secret, while the mask is redrawn — so it is the one quantity in a "masked" column that repeat proving does not blur. **Measurement:** re-run `the_c1_attack_does_not_close_on_c7` with the filler modelled as `hash2(secret, cycle)` — one unknown plus the AIR's own Poseidon relation — instead of nine free scalars, and report the null-space dimension both ways. Separately, generate two proofs of one note and diff the recovered column-9 structure.

**3. Repeated proving of one note compounds over time, and the flow guarantees repeats.**
Every entry in the ledger prices a *single* proof. The full C7 wire publishes ~1,600 felts against 1,280 mask cells (the `published-system-is-overdetermined` entry establishes this), i.e. **+320 net equations per proof against a witness that never changes**. Two proofs of the same note give ~640, ten give ~3,200, on ~28 witness unknowns. And repeats are not hypothetical: `prepareUnshieldJobV4` (`apps/web/lib/privacy/pool/unshieldEphemeral.ts:413`) runs the ~2-minute proving step and can throw afterwards on an aged-out root, affordability, the self-deposit guard or the spend-funder guard; the proof is generated inside the prepare and a retry re-enters it, and the wasm draws a fresh mask each call (`stark/src/lib.rs:428`). An abandoned upload already leaves its chunks and its public inputs on chain permanently (`public-inputs-in-verifier-instruction-data`). **Why nobody listed it:** every sweeper measured a transaction, not a history. **Measurement:** count, across recorded devnet history, how many distinct proof-buffer uploads exist per eventual nullifier — any nullifier with ≥2 uploads is a note whose published system is a multiple of the one the margin was computed for. Then run the rank measurement on the *union* of two proofs' equations for one witness.

**4. Nobody has ever formed the joint C1+C3 (or C1+C3+C6) system, though v3 publishes them together over one witness.**
`proof-bytes-c3-c6-unmeasured` marks C3 and C6 unknown *individually*. But a v3 spend uploads a C1 proof and a C3 proof in the same flow, signed by the same payer, and the values are explicitly shared — C1's public inputs are `[nullifier, commitment]` and C3's leaf *is* that commitment. A deposit adds C6 over the same leaf. So the real object is a single system over one witness with three sets of openings, and the only measurement anyone has run is C1 alone. **Why nobody listed it:** the proof-bytes sweeper worked circuit by circuit. **Measurement:** clone `air_aware_recovery_c1.rs`, add C3's opening and AIR-equality rows to the same matrix with the commitment as a shared variable, and report whether the joint rank recovers anything C1 alone did not — in particular the leaf index, which C3 carries and C1 does not.

**5. The browser extension is an entire unswept surface, and it ships an AI agent that posts the wallet's balance and the user's free text to Groq and Google.**
The client sweeper scoped itself to `apps/web/**`. `apps/extension/src/shared/services/` holds a complete parallel stack — `denominatedPool.ts`, `noteCrypto.ts`, `stark.ts`, `starkWasmData.ts`, `wallet.ts`, `sessionCrypto.ts`, `relay.ts`, `subscriptionVault.ts`, `stealth.ts` — none of it examined for storage, RPC, or seed handling, and the ledger already knows from elsewhere that this build still *mints* v3-routed notes. Worse, it carries `services/ai/`: `agent.ts:280` POSTs to `https://api.groq.com/openai/v1/chat/completions` and `:318` to `https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/…gemini-1.5-flash:streamGenerateContent`, with a system prompt built by `buildContextString` (`:95-108`) that appends `WALLET: <n> SOL`; `AIContext` (`:84-88`) declares `balance`, `walletAddress` and `marketData`. `marketData.ts:27` and `:68` call `api.alternative.me` and `price.jup.ag` from the same context, and `jupiter.ts` and `price.ts` add `quote-api.jup.ag`, `token.jup.ag`, `api.coingecko.com`, `raw.githubusercontent.com` and `arweave.net`. So a privacy wallet makes outbound requests, from the user's IP, to five third parties who are named in no privacy policy, one of which receives the user's balance and whatever they type ("can I move my shielded note to X"). The `walletAddress` field is declared and plumbed even though today's `buildContextString` does not emit it — one line from being sent. **Measurement:** open the extension, ask the agent one question, and capture the POST body; list every field that leaves. Then diff the extension's third-party origins against `apps/web/app/privacy/page.tsx`, which already admits it under-discloses.

**6. The OS clipboard and the share sheet carry bearer material, and on the web nothing clears them.**
Every "closed" note-transfer verdict assumes the sealed blob's only carrier is an out-of-band channel the user chose. In practice the app puts spend authority on the system clipboard: `apps/web/components/pay/SendForm.tsx:545` writes the sealed note, `SubscribePanel.tsx:994` writes the licence key, `ReceivePanel.tsx:376` writes `identity.meta` — with **no clearing** on any of them. On mobile the same values go to the clipboard *and* to `Share.share` (`denominated-notes.tsx:235`, `denominated-transfer.tsx:300`), which hands the blob to whatever app the user picks. Mobile at least clears after 60 s (`denominated-notes.tsx:227`); the web does not clear at all. The clipboard is readable by any focused page, by any extension with clipboard permission, and on Windows and macOS it syncs to clipboard history and to the user's other devices. **Why nobody listed it:** the client sweeper drew its boundary at origin storage and the network; the clipboard is neither. **Measurement:** copy a sealed note in the web app, then read `navigator.clipboard.readText()` from an unrelated origin's devtools and from Windows clipboard history (Win+V) after 10 minutes. Anything still there is a bearer instrument sitting outside every store the L5 rework sealed.

**7. A failed subscribe and a later withdrawal of the same note share one ephemeral key — deliberately — and the justification only covers spends, not attempts.**
`apps/web/lib/privacy/pool/subscribeEphemeral.ts:19-27` reuses `deriveUnshieldEphemeral(seed, pool, leafIndex)` verbatim so that stranded proof-buffer rent stays inside `recoverFloat`'s two-derivation sweep, and defends it with "A note is spent once, so the two uses of the key are mutually exclusive anyway." That is true of *spends* and false of *attempts*: the same header concedes "a subscribe that dies between the pre-fund and the buffer close strands ~1 SOL" — which is an ephemeral with a public funding transaction and up to ~150 chunk uploads on it. When the user later withdraws that note, the withdrawal is signed by **the same address**. So an abandoned subscription attempt and the eventual withdrawal are joined by a key, and `recoverFloat` adds a third edge to the recovery destination. Shield and unshield *are* properly domain-separated (`shieldEphemeral.ts:75` `p01:web:shield-ephemeral:v1` vs `unshieldEphemeral.ts:85` `p01:web:unshield-ephemeral:v1`), which is why nobody looked further — the one collision is the one that was chosen on purpose. **Measurement:** for each unshield ephemeral on devnet, count prior transactions on that address before the withdrawal's own pre-fund. Any non-zero count is an abandoned attempt joined to a landed spend.

**8. The crowd is mostly treasury inventory, and treasury leaves are classifiable — so `note_count` overstates every user's anonymity set.**
`anonymity-set-size` computes k = leaves − spends and calls it a ceiling. It is a much looser ceiling than stated, because a large share of those leaves are operator-deposited inventory that an observer can separate out: they are funded by the float, they arrive on a 4-hourly cadence (`.github/workflows/restock-inventory.yml`, `cron: '41 */4 * * *'`), and — the part no sweeper saw — they are submitted from **GitHub Actions runner IP ranges**, while user deposits come from residential IPs, so the shared RPC provider partitions them for free. The workflow's own header shows the team already reasons this way ("A cron at :41 makes 'the deposits near :41' a recognisable class") and mitigates the minute but not the class. Every user's real crowd is `note_count` minus identifiable inventory, and no probe computes that. **Measurement:** classify every leaf by its funder (float vs. relayed buyer) and report k with inventory removed; separately, check whether the restock's 4-hour cadence survives the random hold well enough to be un-clusterable, on the actual landed slots rather than in the comment.

**9. Network-layer observers are missing entirely — the ledger stops at "the RPC provider sees both halves."**
`rpc-same-ip-sees-both-halves` is about Helius. Below that sit parties nobody named: the user's ISP and any on-path observer see the **TLS SNI and DNS** for the deployment's domain, for the relayer host, and for Groq/Google/Jupiter from the extension. Connecting to the spend relayer's hostname at all is a disclosure that this IP performed a shielded withdrawal, independent of everything on chain, and it happens before any proof exists. There is no proxy, Tor, or alternate-endpoint option anywhere in the tree. **Why nobody listed it:** it is below the application layer, so it fell between the client sweeper and the metadata sweeper. **Measurement:** capture one relayed withdrawal at the packet level and list every hostname resolved and every SNI sent, with timestamps, then check whether the sequence alone identifies the operation.

**10. Fifteen published SDK packages were never swept, and third-party integrators inherit whatever is in them.**
The sweepers touched `packages/stark-prover` and `packages/privacy-sdk` (the L18 CU-price counter). `packages/` also holds `pay-core`, `zk-sdk`, `zkspl-sdk`, `specter-js`, `specter-sdk`, `privacy-toolkit`, `react-native-zk`, `whitelist-sdk`, `auth-sdk`, `merchant-sdk`, `p01-js`, `rpc-config`, `arcium-sdk`, `ui`. Memory records these were republished to npm and that a bypass-2FA token was never revoked. A leak in a published SDK is a leak in every integrator's product, with no way to recall it — and the one place anyone did look found a live regression (`upload-protocol.ts:410`, `microLamports: i + 1`) that the mobile fix had missed. `apps/mugen`, `apps/weekly-update` and a committed `P01-Extension-v0.1.1.zip` are likewise unexamined. **Measurement:** for each published package, list every outbound request, every persisted key, and every value that reaches a log or an error message; then diff each against the repo source it claims to be built from, since the shipped-blob provenance problem is already known to be unpinned.

**11. Unlinkability is not a property a spend keeps — the set decays, and nothing re-measures a landed spend.**
Every probe verdict is a snapshot taken once and frozen in a fixture. But the anonymity set of a *past* spend moves: as the remaining crowd is spent, the candidate deposits for an old withdrawal shrink toward one, and a note that sits unspent while everything around it drains becomes uniquely identified by elimination. `p11-measurement-decay` catches one instance of this (the audit tool's own cap) and reads it as a tooling defect; the general form — **a green measured today is not a claim about tomorrow, and nothing in CI re-runs a fixture against present chain state** — is not in the ledger. Two of today's greens are absences (P11's 0-of-94, P10's "not swept yet"), and an absence is the one kind of measurement that expires silently. **Measurement:** re-run the full probe suite against the *same* recorded spends monthly and record the verdict history, not just the latest verdict; report, per landed spend, how its candidate-deposit count has changed since it landed.

**12. Several "closed" verdicts are conditional on facts about the two current pools, and no test holds those facts.**
Threaded through the skeptic notes but never given a row of its own: `recipient-cleartext-v4`'s offset 115 and `totalLen: 147` are correct only at `tree_depth = 15`; the `directions` bits carry zero information only while every leaf is in bucket 0, and the test that claims to guard that boundary asserts four hard-coded literals (`spend_root.rs:239-251`) and can never fire; the v3 SPL owner gap is latent only while both pools are native SOL; the relayed path exists only above a 0.5 SOL denomination. **A future change is the failure mode here** — a depth-16 pool, an SPL pool, a second denomination, a second relayer, or mainnet each re-opens a channel someone has already written down as closed, and none of those transitions has a tripwire. **Measurement:** for each closed verdict, write down the fact it depends on and add one assertion that reads that fact from chain state rather than from a literal; the cheapest is `pool.next_leaf_index < 4096` and `pool.token_mint == 11111…`, both one RPC call.

**13. The ledger's own citations are a channel, because a false citation is what stops the next reader looking.**
Three of the skeptic passes found prose that a reader would take as evidence and that is not true of the code: `compact_proof.rs:531-535`'s "authenticated and then unused" mirror rows (they are consumed, `verify.rs:1937-1944`), `poolHandlers.ts:645-647` describing L4 as the current design of the handler that closed it, and the 48-50-second purchase-to-deposit figure, which appears in five files and has no artifact behind it in any of them. The `stale-documentation-poolresolvespent` entry names two of these and rules itself "not a channel." It is the right ruling and the wrong conclusion: today's finding was that a load-bearing citation in the audit tool had no code anywhere in history, so **prose that reads as measurement is the mechanism by which this ledger becomes false while staying green.** Before engraving anything, the claim that "nothing leaks" should be forbidden from resting on any sentence that cites a comment. **Measurement:** mechanise the check that `topologyInvariants.test.ts` and `depositPublicity.test.ts` already do for two properties — for every comment in `lib/privacy`, `programs/`, and `stark/src` that asserts a privacy property, require a test name or a recorded artifact in the same block, and fail the build on a bare assertion.
